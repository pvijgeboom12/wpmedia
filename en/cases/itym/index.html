ITYM

Explaining cybersecurity to people with no time for it

ITYM is a Dutch IT and cybersecurity partner for SMEs. The story was technical, the audience busy and the offer broad. Since spring 2026 we have been building positioning, research, a quickscan as the way in, a clean CRM and the campaigns around it.

The company

ITYM provides IT management, cybersecurity and managed services to Dutch companies with up to 250 employees. The team knows its technology. For the owner of a twenty-person company, cybersecurity often feels abstract, until something goes wrong.

The offer was broad, the website spoke the language of IT, and the CRM held hundreds of contacts nobody had a clear view of anymore.

The brief

Make the story clear and build a route to a conversation.

Not another sales pitch, but a reason to talk. With facts about the reader’s own company, a clear next step and a CRM that shows who is really interested.

What we built

  • Positioning and information architecture

    We brought the offer back to four pillars: Secure, Manage, Modernize and Partner. hello.itym.nl became the entrance to the funnel, not a second security site. First there was one A3 with brand and structure. Only then did we build.

  • Research as a brand

    Under the name Digitale Weerbaarheidsmonitor Nederland (Dutch Digital Resilience Monitor) we screened the public mail and web settings of 640 Dutch domains. In June 2026 a report followed on the basic cyber hygiene of 160 SMEs. Facts instead of fear: that changes the tone of a first conversation.

  • A quickscan as the way in

    The same public checks as in the research, but for the visitor’s own domain. The result is a Security Score with open issues and quick fixes. The reader gets something of value first, and ITYM gets a concrete reason to get in touch.

  • A CRM you can trust again

    We cleaned and enriched HubSpot. 623 companies and 639 contacts got a mail score for their own domain. Dead domains were flagged and duplicates removed. An A, B and C tiering on sector and vulnerability now decides who gets attention first.

  • Campaigns built on own data

    A direct mailing with the real findings for each company’s own domain. LinkedIn ads on a company list of 623 domains. And a three-mail sequence around the research, with a switch of sender. The new Dutch Cybersecurity Act (NIS2) gave a concrete reason to act.

  • Measurement without the hassle

    A measurement plan for GA4, HubSpot and Hotjar, and a GDPR-proof consent setup with Consent Mode v2 on both sites. So we see what works, within the rules.

What we learned

Research opens doors a sales pitch keeps shut. An owner who reads that more than half of his sector does not stop email spoofing wants to know where he stands himself. That is a different conversation from “we do cybersecurity too”.

The second mail did the work. Every click and reply came after the personal follow-up, from a different sender and in a different tone. The first mail set the scene, the second got the response. In later sequences we keep the first mail short and put the warmth in the second.

The hardest lesson was in the last step. People clicked through to the quickscan, but in the first two weeks nobody completed it. The landing page did not deliver on the promise in the mail. Since then there is a fixed rule: before any campaign goes out, someone walks the whole route once, from mail to submitted form. If that is not crystal clear, the campaign does not ship.

Data quality beats volume. Part of the list turned out to be unreachable. A different sender did not fix that: a test with 33 sent mails produced zero deliveries. We now enrich and check before sending. That is how 493 contacts moved from a generic info@ address to a personal one.
